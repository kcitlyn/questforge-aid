# 90-Second Demo / Loom Script

A short screen-recording is the single highest-leverage thing you can add: it
turns the *invisible* engineering (the safety layer, the human-in-the-loop
design) into the thing the reviewer actually remembers. Keep it under 90s.

**Setup before recording:** open the published Lovable URL in a clean browser
window, sound on (so the reviewer hears the synthesized audio), Session Log
empty. Have the boar scenario pre-loaded (it's the default).

---

## The script (read it naturally, don't sound rehearsed)

**[0:00–0:10] — The problem, in one breath**
> "This is Quest Craft Co-Pilot. The hardest moment for a new Game Master isn't
> prep — it's when kids do something totally unplanned mid-game and everyone's
> looking at you. That's the moment this tool is for."

**[0:10–0:25] — The required demo, live**
> "Here's Quest Craft's own example: the kids beat the Stormbristle Boar, and
> instead of honoring Artemis they want to sell it for profit. I hit Get
> Suggestions…"

*(Click. While it loads:)*
> "…and in a couple seconds I get three story paths — not three flavors of the
> same idea, three different tones."

**[0:25–0:45] — Walk the output (this is the required-elements checklist)**
> "Each path honors their choice instead of punishing it, and each has a
> consequence that pays off later — that's the part that keeps a campaign
> coherent. There's narration I can literally read aloud, with a delivery hint,
> and a dice hook tied to Quest Craft's own Strength/Wisdom/Charisma system."

**[0:45–1:00] — The differentiator: safety + human control**
> "Two things I care about most. First — I never have to trust the AI blindly:
> every response is checked by a *second, independent* safety pass before I see
> it, and if that check can't run, it tells me to use my judgment rather than
> pretending it's fine. Second — nothing is ever forced. Every suggestion is
> accept, revise, or ignore. The human GM always decides."

**[1:00–1:15] — One interactive beat (pick ONE, live)**
> *(Type in the vibe box:)* "I can also steer it — say, 'make it sillier, and
> the merchant is secretly a god in disguise' — and regenerate."
>
> *OR* *(Accept an outcome, then click Call back now in the Session Log:)*
> "When I accept an outcome it goes to my Session Log, and later — one click —
> the tool weaves that thread back into the current scene. That's 'a consequence
> that matters later' as an actual feature, not a promise."

**[1:15–1:30] — Close on judgment, not features**
> "The whole thing is built around Quest Craft's real adoption barrier —
> confidence, not interest. It's fast enough to use live, it's age-aware, and it
> keeps the adult in control. Everything's in the repo — the prompt, the safety
> architecture, and the guardrail tests. Thanks for watching."

---

## Notes
- If the live model is slow or errors, **do not hide it** — but record when
  you've confirmed it's working. A broken demo video is worse than no video.
- Don't narrate the UI ("here's a button"). Narrate the *decisions* — why two
  layers, why accept/revise/ignore, why offscreen framing. That's what signals
  you'd be a good AI hire.
- Mention the tech stack once, briefly, only if it feels natural: "built in
  Lovable, hardened in Cursor." Don't dwell on tools.
